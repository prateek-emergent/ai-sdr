Summary

What the agent gained

34 commits, 2,238 lines added in the core modules (voice/, agent/, prompts, settings), 8 new core modules, tests from 0 to 60.

0.50 sLLM first token, median, with the cached promptwas 0.58 s (pod A/B)
64%simulated calls that keep to the approved claimswas 46%
92%simulated calls where a "yes" gets bookedwas 79%
−31%"Perfetto/Benissimo"-type words per call1.39 → 0.96

Improved

  • Knows who she is calling: the customer card from HubSpot is read while the phone rings, split into what she may say and what only steers her.
  • Speaks the caller's language: Italian or English per call, with a mid-call switch to a twin agent that has its own voice and speech-to-text.
  • Sounds more like a person: prompt v4, examples that match the approved claims, no stock enthusiasm words, room tone, typing during lookups, a filler only when a lookup is slow, noise cancellation.
  • Answers a little faster: the prompt and tools (10.7k of 11.1k tokens) are read from cache; shorter sentence pieces go to the voice; barge-in stops her sooner; replies are capped at 400 tokens.
  • Survives outages: sticky LLM fallback; on 21 Oct the LiteLLM key ran out and the pod kept answering on the Emergent route.
  • Measurable: every end-of-turn decision, every model that answered and every turn's timings are in the call report.

Not improved, and why

  • The caller's median wait stayed about 1.2 s (1.20 s on 20 Oct, 1.23–1.26 s now). It was already near the floor of a cascaded STT → LLM → TTS pipeline: end-of-turn ~0.4–0.6 s, the LLM's first sentence ~0.8 s, first audio ~0.1 s.
  • The 1.2 s end-of-turn cap was kept on purpose: on 121 real calls a shorter cap saved 0.02–0.06 s per turn but cut people off in 1–5% of turns.
  • One "Perfetto" still slips through now and then; the ban halves the habit, it doesn't remove it.

Low-level design 5

Bilingual calls: switching language mid-call

A LiveKit handoff to a twin agent

new
sequenceDiagram
  participant C as Caller
  participant A as Giulia (it)
  participant K as CallController
  participant B as Giulia twin (en)
  C->>A: "Scusi, possiamo parlare in inglese?"
  A->>A: tool switch_language("en"), says nothing first
  A->>K: set_language("en"), event language_switch

Low-level design 8

Bad lines and silence

One counter per call, handled in code

kept from the pickup; now follows the call's language
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Talking
  Talking --> CheckIn1: 10 s of silence
  CheckIn1 --> Talking: caller answers
  CheckIn1 --> CheckIn2: 6 s after her line
  CheckIn2 --> Talking: caller answers
  CheckIn2 --> Close: 6 s more, "we'll call back later"
  Talking --> AskCallback: more than 5 retries (check-ins and "can you repeat?")
  AskCallback --> Talking: caller gives a time
  AskCallback --> Close: no answer in 10 s
  Close --> [*]

The "sorry, could you repeat that?" that covers an LLM failure doesn't count as a bad line, giving up never overwrites an outcome already recorded, and every timer belongs to the call's scope, so nothing outlives the call.

Low-level design 10

Process and deploy safety

New code without dropping calls

kept from the pickup, used on every push
flowchart LR
  GIT["git pull on the pod"] --> VER["VERSION file changes"]
  VER --> SUP["supervisor checks every 15 s"]
  SUP --> NEW["start a new worker on the new code"]
  SUP --> OLD["old worker drains: finishes its calls, takes no new ones"]
  NEW --> HEALTH["/health: version, ready"]
  • One supervisor per machine (file lock); model files downloaded before the first start; restart with backoff, reset after 600 s of health.
  • A .env change rolls the worker the same way (that is how the Groq key took effect).
  • Before every push: 60 tests, the prompt parity check and a secret scan; VERSION = the last code commit.
